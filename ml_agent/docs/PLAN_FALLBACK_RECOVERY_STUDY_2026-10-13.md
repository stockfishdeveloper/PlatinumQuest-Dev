# Post-batch general fallback and recovery study

Prepared from observed Cube/Duplex failures; do not modify the running component
batch. This is a general controller investigation, not a named maneuver or
map-specific fix. No policy training or checkpoint promotion.

The inspected Duplex prefix has49falls, all observed during A2 fallback.
Some repeated pre-fall poses are already many units below the platform. That
does not prove the earlier checked actions were safe or caused the fall: bridge
inputs are delayed, so trace the whole transition instead of attributing the
fall to its immediately logged command. Geometry-only and legacy actors also
fall frequently on this seed; retaining all three results matters.

1. From the closed native/decision archives, delimit each life by actual native
   respawn counters. Recover the last supported approach, entry into flight,
   checked/fallback transitions and reasons, actual pending controls, goal and
   inventory. Compare checked forecasts only across continuous64ms observation
   intervals, never across a warp or control gap. Save representative early and
   repeated failures without choosing only favorable trajectories.
2. Distinguish inability to predict a safe control from time spent on gem/global
   alternatives before landing/braking/resource continuations. Current one-second
   search deadline is not a whole-decision bound. Measure candidate coverage and
   actual total cost before deciding a budget change.
3. Evaluate an ordinary braking or supported landing backup from archived BEFORE
   states with the same physics/ambiguity checks. A safe backup need not make
   immediate gem progress; account for its opportunity cost and eventual legal
   rescue. Never describe a neutral input as inherently safe, or classify an
   unsafe actor fallback as verified merely because other candidates failed.
4. Where an exact short native fixture restoration passes pose, velocity, spin,
   body parameters, inventory/effects, camera and pending-control checks, replay
   original versus proposed general recovery controls in disposable sessions.
   A mismatch remains a failed reproduction, not an exact test. Short restored
   attempts are engineering diagnostics, never complete-round scores.
5. If recovery-first/reserved-budget or fallback verification is supported by
   these results, implement in the maintained controller and tests after the
   freeze. Declare a fresh ten-map seed/settings/version before a full comparison.
   Keep the original outcomes, costs and any new unfavorable map results.

The physical student protocol remains a separate route to useful general
prediction. It is not required to explain away the fall loop and is not deployed.
KOTM is one equal-weight map and never a special veto over either investigation.
