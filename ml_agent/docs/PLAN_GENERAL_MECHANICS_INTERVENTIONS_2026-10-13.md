## Research review during the fixed native comparison

Reviewed primary papers on 2026-10-13. These motivate experiments; their results do not establish accuracy, generalization or real-time cost in Marble Blast.

- [FIGNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03574) models interactions between mesh faces. Its authors identify a weakness of vertex-only collision representations: contact can occur well inside a large face, far from every vertex. This supports retaining exact triangle/edge information and testing face-interior contacts, rather than replacing our verified collision map with vertex samples. Our current attention student is not FIGNet and has no learned graph message passing.
- [Act-FIGNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12151v1) adds applied force/torque inputs to mesh-based dynamics and evaluates predicted sequences fed back into the model. It uses pose history and tests control in peg insertion. Its robot forces are different from the game's delayed Move, jump eligibility, inventory activation and effect timers. Our input and state-transition contracts must encode those game mechanisms explicitly; the paper's performance numbers are not expected Marble Blast results.
- [Allen et al., CoRL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/allen23a.html) show graph simulators learning discontinuous contact dynamics in their evaluated systems. This supports investigating a general contact model rather than enumerating tricks. It does not remove the need to measure our wall impacts, spin, support transitions and edge contacts separately.
- [GNS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/sanchez-gonzalez20a.html) reports message-passing depth and training noise as important for long rollout quality in its particle-based systems. This reinforces the distinction between our one-step development score and a useful autoregressive world model. Any noise/history experiment must be separately declared and must respect exact body, control-queue and effect discontinuities.

Candidate architecture after verified intervention data: cache the complete static map with exact face/source-edge/material identities; provide dynamic marble state, spin, recent state/control history, cached contact state, body parameters and effect/inventory clocks; compute action-conditioned marble-to-surface interactions; predict the next physical state plus explicitly modeled discrete changes. Geometry is static, but interaction queries must update at every predicted state so a distant landing surface becomes relevant as the simulated marble approaches it. Spatial indexing may accelerate exact queries without dropping source geometry or hiding omitted interactions.

This is an inference from the papers and our native audits, not an implemented graph model. Keep the geometry available to both long-range destination search and local dynamics. A short-range interaction graph can approximate local physical causality while the planner evaluates distant destinations through rollouts; a local graph alone does not give the hunting policy global route knowledge. Preserve full-map queries and measure missed collisions, first-contact error, multi-step position/velocity/spin tails and actual natural-round collection before deployment.
